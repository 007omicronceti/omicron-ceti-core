# How Mira Works: Pulsation, Molecular Veiling, Superwinds & Magnetic Eruptions (`Mira A`)

**Omicron Ceti A (Mira A)** is a low-to-intermediate-mass star (~1.18 $M_\odot$) currently traversing the **Thermally Pulsing Asymptotic Giant Branch (TP-AGB)**—the same late evolutionary stage our own Sun will enter in roughly 5 billion years. Understanding Mira A explains how intermediate-mass stars enrich the galaxy with carbon, oxygen, and silicate dust before shedding their outer layers into planetary nebulae.

---

## 1. Internal Architecture of a TP-AGB Star

Unlike a stable main-sequence star burning hydrogen smoothly in its core, Mira A is stratified into three extreme zones:
1. **Degenerate Carbon-Oxygen (C/O) Core:** Inert, Earth-sized, containing ~0.6 $M_\odot$ of electron-degenerate carbon and oxygen ash.
2. **Alternating Nuclear-Burning Shells:** A thin helium-burning shell wrapped beneath a hydrogen-burning shell. Roughly every ~10,000–100,000 years, the helium shell ignites explosively in a **thermal pulse (helium shell flash)**, driving deep convective dredge-up episodes that haul freshly synthesized carbon and *s*-process heavy elements to the surface.
3. **Hyper-Extended Convective Envelope:** A tenuous hydrogen-rich atmosphere swollen to **330–700 solar radii ($R_\odot$)**—wide enough to engulf the orbits of Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars. Because surface gravity is extraordinarily low, giant convective plumes comparable in size to a significant fraction of the stellar radius boil across its photosphere.

---

## 2. The $\kappa$-Mechanism (Kappa Opacity Valve): What Drives the ~332-Day Cycle

Mira A's radial pulsation (~331.96-day mean period) is powered by a thermodynamic heat-engine valve known as the **$\kappa$-mechanism** (where $\kappa$ denotes radiative opacity):

1. **Compression Phase:** Beneath the visible photosphere lies the **partial ionization zone** of hydrogen and helium. When gravity pulls the envelope inward, gas density and temperature climb.
2. **Opacity Spike (Valve Closes):** Normally, heating a gas makes it more transparent. In the partial ionization zone, however, incoming energy goes into stripping electrons off neutral hydrogen/helium atoms rather than raising kinetic temperature—causing **opacity ($\kappa$) to spike sharply**.
3. **Radiation Trapping & Outward Piston:** With outgoing photons trapped beneath the opaque layer, radiation pressure builds until it forces the bloated outer envelope to expand violently outward.
4. **Expansion, Recombination & Reset (Valve Opens):** As the outer shell expands and cools, ions recombine with free electrons, opacity plummets, trapped heat floods into space, and gravity pulls the envelope back inward to start the next **~332-day** cycle.

## 3. The 1,500× Visual Swing Paradox (Wien Shift + $\text{TiO}$ Molecular Veiling)

A defining feature of Mira variables is the stark mismatch between their **visual light curve** and their **bolometric (total) luminosity**:
* **Visual Flux Change:** Swings from magnitude **~2.0–3.5** at maximum down to **~8.6–10.1** at minimum—a brightness factor of **~1,000× to 1,500×** in human-visible wavelengths (400–700 nm).
* **Total Energy (Bolometric) Change:** Varies by only a factor of **~2×** across the entire cycle.

Two physical effects amplify this modest ~2× energy swing into a dramatic disappearing act:
1. **Wien's Displacement Law:** At maximum light, Mira A's effective temperature reaches **~2,900–3,200 K** (spectral type ~M5e). At minimum light, it cools to **~1,900–2,100 K** (spectral type ~M9e). Because the blackbody peak already sits in the near-infrared, even a ~1,000 K drop shifts the tiny short-wavelength tail steeply out of the visible band.
2. **Titanium Oxide ($\text{TiO}$) Molecular Blanketing:** Below ~2,500 K, **titanium oxide ($\text{TiO}$)**, vanadium oxide ($\text{VO}$), and water vapor ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) condense abundantly in Mira's extended upper atmosphere. $\text{TiO}$ possesses dense electronic absorption bands right across the optical spectrum—acting like a dark molecular curtain that blocks visible light at minimum and dissociates when the star heats back up toward maximum.

## 4. Shock-Driven Dust Levitation & the 2026 ALMA Magnetic Eruption Discovery

### Classical Pulsation-Enhanced Dust-Driven Wind
Each 332-day pulsation sends acoustic/hydrodynamic **shockwaves** rippling outward through Mira's chromosphere, levitating dense gas several stellar radii above the photosphere where temperatures fall below ~1,500 K. There, refractory **silicate and alumina dust grains** condense. Radiation pressure from Mira's intense infrared output pushes on these dust grains, which drag surrounding gas outward via collisional coupling at **~5–10 km/s**, sustaining a mass-loss rate of $\sim 10^{-7}$ to $3 \times 10^{-7}\,M_\odot\,\text{yr}^{-1}$.

### Breakthrough 2026 ALMA Observations: Giant Magnetically Driven Eruptions
Classical dust-driven wind theory struggled to explain why Mira A produces episodic, highly directional mass ejections and localized sub-millimeter atmospheric hotspots. In **August–October 2026**, Wouter Vlemmings et al. published full-polarisation **ALMA Band 6** observations (project `2024.1.01776.S`, baselines up to 14.67 km, angular resolution 1.5–2.6 AU) of the $\text{SiO}\,v=1,\,J=5\rightarrow 4$ maser transition at 215 GHz:
* **Discovery:** ALMA imaged a **highly magnetized, loop-like mass ejection** traced by strongly linearly and circularly polarized SiO masers extending **120 milliarcseconds (~12 AU, or ~8 stellar radii)** from Mira A.
* **Kinematics:** The eruptive loop expands at ~17 km/s along the ejection axis, consistent with a ballistic launch velocity near **~30 km/s** (close to Mira A's escape velocity) from a localized 230 GHz surface hotspot (~2,340 K).
* **Astrophysical Implication:** These observations provide direct evidence of a **stellar coronal mass ejection (CME) analog on an AGB star**, demonstrating that **magnetic eruptions can dominate episodic mass loss** from evolved cool giants alongside dust-driven winds.
* **Primary Paper:** Vlemmings, W. et al. (2026), *"A giant magnetic eruption from the asymptotic giant branch star Mira A"*, *Astronomy & Astrophysics* / arXiv (`arXiv:2608.23695v2`, Oct 19, 2026):

## 5. Mira Variables as Cosmic Distance-Ladder Anchors

In the near-infrared (particularly the *K*-band, 2.2 $\mu$m), Mira variables obey a tight **Period-Luminosity Relation (PLR)**—similar to Cepheids, but brighter in the infrared and present in older stellar populations (including elliptical galaxies where Cepheids are absent). Recent calibrations using **ESA Gaia** data and Mira variables in star clusters provide an independent cross-check on the **Hubble constant ($H_0$)** and the ongoing Hubble tension.
