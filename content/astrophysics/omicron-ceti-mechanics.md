# System Mechanics: Omicron Ceti

The Omicron Ceti system (Mira) is an interacting symbiotic binary governed by extreme mass transfer and supersonic interstellar velocities. 

## Astrometric Telemetry
- **Right Ascension:** 02h 19m 20.79s
- **Declination:** -02° 58' 39.5"
- **Distance:** ~299 Light-Years (92 parsecs)
- **System Velocity:** ~130 km/s (supersonic relative to the ambient interstellar medium)

## Component A: Mira (Red Giant)
- **Classification:** Asymptotic Giant Branch (AGB) Star, Spectral Type M5e-M9e.
- **Radius:** Fluctuates between 332 and 400 Solar Radii (large enough to engulf the orbit of Mars if placed in our solar system).
- **Variability:** 332-day pulsation cycle, driven by the expansion and contraction of its outer envelope. Apparent magnitude shifts violently from +2.0 (bright) to +10.1 (invisible).
- **Mass Loss Rate:** Currently shedding $10^{-7}$ solar masses per year. 

## Component B: VZ Ceti (White Dwarf)
- **Classification:** DA-type White Dwarf.
- **Orbital Elements:** Mean distance of 70 Astronomical Units (AU), orbital period of ~400 Earth years.
- **Accretion Engine:** Mira B orbits entirely within the stellar wind of Mira A. A massive accretion disk of captured gas swirls around the white dwarf, generating intense X-ray fluorescence captured by the Chandra Observatory.

## The Ultraviolet Bow Shock & Wake
Discovered by NASA's GALEX in 2007, Mira's 130 km/s velocity creates a highly compressed bow shock in the direction of its travel. 
- The shed stellar envelope is blown backward into a 13-light-year-long tail. 
- This structure acts as a "fossil record" containing 30,000 years of continuous mass loss, rich in carbon and oxygen isotopes.
