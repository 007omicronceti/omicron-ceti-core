# The Mira AB Symbiotic Binary System (`Mira A` + `Mira B / VZ Ceti`)

Omicron Ceti is not an isolated red giant—it is a wide, interacting **symbiotic binary system (`Mira AB`)** whose two stars are separated by **~0.5–0.6 arcseconds** on the sky (roughly **70–200 AU**, or ~2× Pluto's distance from the Sun) and orbit a common center of mass roughly once every **~498 years**.

---

## 1. Binary Components

* **Mira A ($\omicron$ Ceti A):** Thermally pulsing AGB red giant (~1.18 $M_\odot$, spectral type M5e–M9e, ~332-day pulsation period) losing mass via dense dusty winds and magnetic eruptions.
* **Mira B (`VZ Ceti`):** A hot, compact **white dwarf** (~0.6–0.7 $M_\odot$) first detected spectroscopically by **Alfred Harrison Joy** at Mount Wilson Observatory in **1923** (via anomalous blue continuum and Balmer emission lines during Mira A's faint minima) and directly resolved as a separate point source by the **Hubble Space Telescope** in 1995.

---

## 2. How Wind Accretion Works in `Mira AB`

Because Mira A does not fill its Roche lobe at ~70+ AU separation, mass transfer occurs via **gravitational wind capture (Bondi-Hoyle-Lyttleton / Wind Roche-Lobe Overflow accretion)**:
1. Mira A's slow (~5–10 km/s), dense stellar wind expands outward into the binary system.
2. Mira B's gravitational well bends and funnels a fraction of that wind (~1% of Mira A's total mass loss) into a **luminous gaseous stream/bridge** spiraling toward the white dwarf.
3. Angular momentum prevents the gas from falling straight onto Mira B; instead, it flattens into a hot, turbulent **accretion disk** that exhibits rapid optical/UV **flickering** on timescales of minutes—confirming Mira B's compact white dwarf nature rather than a main-sequence star.

---

## 3. Multi-Wavelength Primary Observations

### A. Hubble Space Telescope (1995–1997) — Resolving the Pair & Gaseous Bridge
Using ESA's **Faint Object Camera (FOC)** aboard Hubble, Margarita Karovska (Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics) and collaborators directly separated Mira A and Mira B in ultraviolet and visible light—revealing an asymmetric, hook-like gaseous plume extending from Mira A's outer atmosphere directly toward Mira B.
* **NASA Science Release:** [Hubble Separates Stars in the Mira Binary System](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/hubble-separates-stars-in-the-mira-binary-system/)
* **NASA Hubble Asset:** [Red Giant Star Mira (Aug 6, 1997)](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/red-giant-star-mira/)
* **ESA/Hubble High-Resolution Archive (`opo9726a`):** [Omicron Ceti — NASA/ESA Hubble Space Telescope](https://esahubble.org/images/opo9726a/)

### B. Chandra X-Ray Observatory (2003–2005) — First X-Ray Image of Interacting AGB/WD Binary
Chandra's ACIS-S imaging spectrometer cleanly separated soft X-ray emission from both Mira A and Mira B—capturing an unexpected soft X-ray outburst on cool giant Mira A alongside steady accretion-disk X-rays on Mira B.
* **Detailed Telemetry & Primary Links:** See [[chandra-xray-data]]

### C. White Dwarf Confirmation via Rapid Accretion Flickering (2010)
Long debated as either a white dwarf or an accreting low-mass main-sequence star, Mira B was decisively identified as a **white dwarf** through high-speed optical photometry of its rapid, large-amplitude accretion flickering (requiring a deep gravitational potential well).
* **Primary Paper:** Sokoloski, J. L. & Bildsten, L. (2010), *"Evidence for the White Dwarf Nature of Mira B"*, *The Astrophysical Journal* **723**, 1188–1194 — [arXiv:1009.2509 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.2509)

### D. ALMA Sub-Millimeter Interferometry (2014–2026)
Long-baseline **ALMA** observations resolved both Mira A's asymmetric radio photosphere and Mira B's surrounding dust/gas disk at tens-of-milliarcseconds precision, culminating in the 2026 discovery of a magnetized SiO-maser eruption loop on Mira A:
* **2026 ALMA Band 6 Polarimetry Paper:** Vlemmings, W. et al. (2026), *"A giant magnetic eruption from the asymptotic giant branch star Mira A"* — [arXiv:2608.23695 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23695) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23695)
