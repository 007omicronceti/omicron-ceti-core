# Chandra X-Ray & Hubble UV Observatory Telemetry: `Mira AB`

NASA's **Chandra X-Ray Observatory** (`CXC Release 05-06`, April 28, 2005) produced the first resolved X-ray image of an interacting red giant / white dwarf binary—capturing an unexpected soft X-ray flare on cool giant **Mira A** alongside steady accretion-disk X-rays around **Mira B**.

## Key Observational Findings

1. **Soft X-Ray Outburst on Mira A (0.3–1.0 keV):** Cool AGB giants (~2,000–3,000 K photospheres) were long assumed to lack solar-like hot X-ray coronae. Chandra's ACIS-S observation detected a dramatic soft X-ray flare directly on Mira A—temporally correlated with UV flaring observed by Hubble—pointing to **magnetic reconnection** or **pulsation-driven shock heating** in Mira A's extended chromosphere (a mechanism now directly corroborated by the 2026 ALMA Band 6 magnetic eruption discovery in `arXiv:2608.23695`).
2. **Steady Accretion X-Rays on Mira B:** Chandra localized steady X-ray emission around **Mira B (VZ Ceti)** generated by rapid particle collisions and shock heating inside the inner boundary layer of the white dwarf's wind-fed accretion disk.
3. **Gaseous Mass-Transfer Bridge:** Paired with Hubble's UV/optical resolution of the ~0.6" separation (~2× Pluto's solar distance), the multi-wavelength data confirmed gravitational siphoning of Mira A's stellar wind directly onto Mira B's disk.
