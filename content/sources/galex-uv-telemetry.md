# GALEX Far-UV Telemetry: Mira's 13-Light-Year Wake (`PIA09958`)

In **August 2007**, NASA's **Galaxy Evolution Explorer (GALEX)** ultraviolet space telescope stunned astrophysicists by revealing a **2-degree-long (~13-light-year)** comet-like far-ultraviolet (FUV: 1344–1786 Å) tail streaming behind **Omicron Ceti (Mira)**. Completely invisible in optical and infrared surveys, the wake preserves a **~30,000-year fossil record** of Mira's mass-loss history as the binary plows supersonically through the local Interstellar Medium (ISM).

| Parameter | Measured Value | Physical Mechanism / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Space Velocity ($v_*$)** | **~130 km/s** (~291,000 mph) | Supersonic motion relative to local ISM (Mach ~10–15) |
| **Angular Length on Sky** | **~2.0 degrees** | ~4× the apparent diameter of the full Moon |
| **Physical Wake Length** | **~13 light-years** | At ~107–110 pc (~350 ly) distance |
| **Dynamical Age of Wake** | **~30,000 years** | Direct chronological archive of recent AGB mass loss |

| **Primary FUV Emission** | **Collisional $H_2$ fluorescence** | Hot post-shock electrons (~$5 \times 10^5$ K) collide with cool molecular hydrogen ($H_2$) stripped from Mira's wind, causing it to glow exclusively in the FUV band |

## Why Only GALEX Saw the Tail

1. **Shock-Heated Stagnation Zone:** As Mira slams into tenuous interstellar gas at 130 km/s, a compressed **bow shock** forms ahead of the star, heating gas to ~$500,000\text{ K}$.
2. **Turbulent Ram-Pressure Stripping:** Cool, dense molecular gas ($H_2$) blown off Mira A sweeps backward around the bow shock into a turbulent Kelvin-Helmholtz wake.
3. **Why Optical Telescopes Missed It:** Mixing between hot post-shock electrons and cool $H_2$ molecules excites electronic Lyman/Werner transitions of molecular hydrogen that radiate almost exclusively in the **far-ultraviolet (1344–1786 Å)**—Earth's atmosphere blocks these wavelengths completely, so it took GALEX's wide-field FUV detector to reveal the 13-light-year streak.
