# The Star of Bethlehem Hypothesis: Omicron Ceti Alignment

The identification of the biblical "Star of Bethlehem" (Matthew 2) is a premier problem in archeoastronomy. While comets (like Halley's in 12 BC) and novae (Chinese records in 5 BC) are frequently proposed, Omicron Ceti represents the most mechanically sound candidate when cross-referenced with planetary tracking of the era.

## The Astronomical Requirements of the Text
The Gospel of Matthew dictates specific mechanical behaviors:
1. **The Appearance:** The star "rose" or appeared in the east.
2. **The Disappearance:** The star was not actively guiding the Magi during their multi-month travel to Jerusalem.
3. **The Reappearance:** The star reappeared and "went before them" to Bethlehem, ultimately appearing to "stand over" a specific location.

## The Mira Alignment Mechanics
Mira is the only naked-eye stellar object that perfectly replicates this cycle.
- **Cycle Timing:** Mira's period is 332 days (approximately 11 months).
- **Magnitude Shift:** At its peak, it reaches magnitude +2.0 (prominent). It then fades to magnitude +10.1 (completely invisible to naked-eye observers), remaining hidden for months before swelling back into visibility. This provides the mechanical explanation for a star that "disappears" and "reappears" exactly when the Magi reach their destination.

## The 7 BC Planetary Conjunction Catalyst
Why were ancient Babylonian/Persian astronomers (Magi) looking at Cetus in the first place? 
- In **7 BC**, a highly rare **triple conjunction of Jupiter and Saturn** occurred in the constellation Pisces (May 29, October 6, and December 1).
- Pisces sits directly adjacent to Cetus. 
- **The Line of Sight:** Observers meticulously charting this 7 BC conjunction would have had their visual field locked on this exact sector of the sky. If Mira reached its peak luminosity (+2.0) during this planetary massing, it would have appeared as a sudden, astonishing new anomaly right beside the conjunction, vanished as it hit its cycle minimum during their travel, and flared back into existence 11 months later.
