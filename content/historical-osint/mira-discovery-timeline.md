# The Cosmological Shift: Discovery of Mira

Prior to 1596, the Aristotelian model of the universe dictated that the celestial sphere was perfect, immutable, and unchanging. Omicron Ceti was the first variable star discovered that shattered this paradigm.

## Chronology of Observation
- **August 13, 1596:** Frisian astronomer David Fabricius observes a star of 3rd magnitude in the constellation Cetus. By October, it vanishes from the sky. Fabricius catalogs it as a "nova" (new star) assuming it has died.
- **February 15, 1603:** Johann Bayer catalogs the star in his *Uranometria*, assigning it the Greek letter Omicron (ο Ceti), unaware of its vanishing nature.
- **1638:** Johannes Holwarda conducts sustained observations, realizing the star disappears and reappears in a predictable ~11-month (332-day) cycle. It is officially classified as the first pulsating variable star.
- **1662:** Johannes Hevelius publishes *Historiola Mirae Stellae*, officially naming it **Mira** (Latin for "wonderful" or "astonishing").
